# Growth Experiment Analysis & Budget Allocation

**Project:** Creator Acquisition & Growth Framework  
**Scenario:** Hypothetical developer-tools SaaS company  
**Status:** Simulated case study — not real campaign results

## Objective

Evaluate creator-led acquisition channels based on qualified signups, product activation, and acquisition efficiency. Use the findings to design a second-round experiment with a $2,000 budget.

## Round 1: Simulated Results

| Metric | YouTube | Instagram | TikTok |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Spend | $500 | $300 | $500 |
| Qualified signups | 25 | 12 | 10 |
| Activated users | 5 | 6 | 4 |
| Cost per qualified signup | $20 | $25 | $50 |
| Activation rate | 20% | 50% | 40% |
| Cost per activated user | $100 | $50 | $125 |

For this exercise, activation means a qualified signup who creates a project and completes a meaningful first product action within seven days.

## Key Findings

**Instagram:** Lowest cost per activated user at $50, making it the strongest candidate for further validation.

**YouTube:** Lowest cost per qualified signup at $20, but its 20% activation rate reduced overall acquisition efficiency.

**TikTok:** Highest cost per activated user at $125. Further testing should focus on creator relevance and content positioning.

These findings are directional, not conclusive, because sample sizes are small.

## Round 2: Proposed Budget

| Channel | Budget | Share | Testing priority |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Instagram | $1,000 | 50% | Validate activation efficiency at higher spend |
| YouTube | $700 | 35% | Improve signup-to-activation conversion |
| TikTok | $300 | 15% | Test more relevant creator segments |
| **Total** | **$2,000** | **100%** | |

## Hypotheses and Success Criteria

### Instagram
- **Hypothesis:** Developer-focused creators can maintain efficient product activation at higher spend.
- **Primary metric:** Cost per activated user.
- **Provisional success criterion:** $60 or less per activated user.
- **Risk:** The initial $50 result may not hold as spending increases.

### YouTube
- **Hypothesis:** More accurate creator messaging and clearer onboarding can improve activation.
- **Primary metric:** Seven-day activation rate.
- **Provisional success criterion:** At least 30%.
- **Risk:** Low activation may reflect audience-product mismatch rather than onboarding friction.

### TikTok
- **Hypothesis:** Creators with more technically relevant audiences can improve acquisition efficiency.
- **Primary metric:** Cost per activated user.
- **Provisional success criterion:** $100 or less per activated user.
- **Risk:** A limited budget may not generate enough activations for reliable conclusions.

## Measurement Plan

Track campaign spend, creator-level referrals, qualified signups, seven-day activations, activation rates, and cost per activated user.

Use consistent campaign tracking parameters, a shared activation definition, and comparable observation windows across channels.

## Decision Framework

- **Scale cautiously:** When activation efficiency meets provisional targets and remains consistent across additional creators and cohorts.
- **Iterate:** When signup acquisition is efficient but activation is weak; investigate audience fit, messaging, and onboarding.
- **Pause or redesign:** When activation efficiency remains poor after a diagnostic test.

Release budgets in stages and reassess performance before increasing spending.

## Limitations

This is a hypothetical portfolio exercise. All results, budgets, and success thresholds are simulated.

Sample sizes are too small to establish statistical confidence. No causal conclusions about creator messaging, onboarding, or channel effectiveness can be drawn without further controlled testing.

## Recommendation

Prioritize Instagram for further validation while retaining YouTube as an optimization opportunity and TikTok as a limited diagnostic experiment.

The objective is to identify creator acquisition approaches that generate activated users efficiently and repeatably.
